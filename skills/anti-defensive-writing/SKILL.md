# Anti Defensive Writing — Academic Writing Principles

Adapted from Adkid-Zephyr's Anti-Defensive Writing at revision
`82d31402caa0db1790026824399044538483171b`, under MIT. See
[UPSTREAM_LICENSE](UPSTREAM_LICENSE). This workbench adaptation replaces rules
about suppressing weaknesses or changing evaluation criteria with explicit
evidence and reporting requirements. It is not an unchanged upstream release.

## Start with the contribution and its evidence

Identify the research question, the central contribution, and the observations
or derivations that support it. Organize the argument around this connection.
For comparative, replication, or negative-result studies, the contribution can
be a well-supported finding or boundary; do not invent superiority or novelty.

Use the requested language and retain the manuscript's submission requirements.
Keep local edits local. Restructure the full argument when requested or needed
for the agreed task, preserving the author's meaning and all material findings.
When another writing workflow handles drafting, language, or document format,
use this skill as its argument-and-framing pass.

## Replace defensive padding with precise statements

- Lead paragraphs with the finding, question, or argument they develop.
- Remove apologies, repeated disclaimers, stacked hedges, and imagined objections
  when they add no evidence, condition, or useful distinction.
- State the actual scope positively: identify the system, conditions, sample,
  and metric studied. Keep a negative statement when it conveys a real exclusion,
  failed result, conceptual distinction, or correction.
- Use claim strength that matches the evidence. A plausible explanation remains
  a hypothesis; association remains association; simulation remains simulation.
- Treat phrases such as "only", "limited", and "does not" as cues to inspect
  meaning, not banned words. Keep them when they carry quantitative or logical
  information. Remove broad self-judgments that exceed the observed finding.

Keep facts, numerical values, units, citations, comparison conditions, uncertainty,
and author attribution intact. Never fabricate a mechanism, significance level,
baseline, generalization, or citation to make the prose more persuasive.

## Present experiments and unfavorable findings fairly

Give each experiment a clear role: test a claim, examine a mechanism, compare
alternatives, test a boundary, or challenge an explanation. A mechanism claim
needs relevant evidence; a performance gain alone does not establish its cause.

Report unfavorable results that affect a claim, interpretation, comparison, or
reader decision. Preserve specified outcomes, denominators, baselines, and
evaluation conditions. Do not select a favorable subset or redefine success
after seeing the results to conceal a failed comparison. Any justified change
in question, metric, or analysis must be explicit and distinguished from the
original evaluation.

Describe the measured tradeoff and its conditions. Explain its cause only when
supported. Narrow or withdraw an unsupported claim. Move redundant or tangential
presentation only when the relevant evidence remains accessible and the main
text remains accurate. A negative finding may itself be the central contribution.

## Put qualifications where readers need them

Consolidate repeated caveats. Keep a condition next to a claim whenever separating
them would mislead, including in an abstract, topic sentence, or conclusion.
Place fuller methodological limits and unresolved questions in the appropriate
section, following journal or institutional requirements.

For an abstract or introduction, connect the problem, relevant gap, approach,
and supported result. Do not claim all prior work fails without a source-backed
comparison. End a conclusion with the supported takeaway and its necessary scope.
For reviewer responses, address the actual comment, explain the evidence or
change, and identify manuscript locations. Admit a demonstrated error directly.

## Deliver the requested revision

Check the revised passage against its inputs: the claim-evidence relation,
material unfavorable findings, quantities, citations, and necessary conditions
must survive. Flag the specific evidence gap when it prevents a requested claim.
Return the revised text in the requested format; give brief reasons for material
changes when useful or requested. Do not add a fixed audit or checklist to every
writing task. Publication or sending a response follows the user's authorization.
